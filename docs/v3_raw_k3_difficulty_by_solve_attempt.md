## Metric Definitions

The structural metrics come from the V3 balanced level metadata generated by `sokoban_memory/boxoban.py`.

- `wall_density`: fraction of 10x10 board cells that are walls.
- `player_reachable_ratio`: fraction of non-wall cells reachable from the initial player position while treating boxes as blocked.
- `initial_legal_push_count`: number of legal box pushes available in the initial state.
- `difficulty_score`: weighted structural and solver-informed difficulty score:

```text
0.35 * (1 - player_reachable_ratio)
+ 0.25 * wall_density
+ 0.25 * (1 - min(initial_legal_push_count, 12) / 12)
+ 0.15 * solver_component
```

`solver_component` is `min(solver_min_pushes, 80) / 80` when the bounded solver found a solution; otherwise it defaults to `0.6`.

## Interpretation

The first-attempt solved group is structurally the easiest by the metadata score. It has the lowest average difficulty score, the highest reachable ratio, and many initial legal pushes. This suggests that one-shot raw planning is strongest when the initial board gives the model broad player mobility and multiple plausible push choices.

The attempt-2 group is more constrained: it has the highest average wall density, a lower reachable ratio, and fewer legal pushes. Same-level raw trajectory memory appears to recover some of these levels after the first failure.

The attempt-3 group is small, so its averages are noisier. Its average difficulty score is lower than the attempt-2 group, but those levels still required two failures before success. That pattern suggests that static board difficulty is not the only factor; retry success can also depend on whether the previous raw trajectories expose a locally fixable planning mistake.

The never-solved group is the hardest overall by `difficulty_score`. It has the lowest reachable ratio and the fewest initial legal pushes, even though its wall density is not the highest. In this 48-level combined view, limited maneuverability and limited initial push options are more diagnostic of persistent failure than wall density alone.
